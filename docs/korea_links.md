Generated by `python pipeline/verify_korea.py` + `python pipeline/korea_links_doc.py` (numbers: `docs/korea_links_check.json`). Window: every hour 2026-01-01T00Z – 2030-12-31T23Z (43,824 h).

**Definition (same as the app):** a link hour = the Earth's centre is above the site's real terrain horizon (2 m observer) **and** the Moon's centre is ≥ 10° above the ground station's horizon. Direct link only: no relay, no link budget, no antenna schedule or availability. Stations: KDSA (Yeoju, 35 m; NAIF `kdsa01.tf`, use pending), DSN Goldstone DSS-14, Madrid DSS-63, Canberra DSS-43 (DSN 810-005 module 301 Rev. P).

**Reading it.** When the Earth is visible from a site, the three DSN 70 m antennas together see the Moon about
98–99 % of the time, so a 4th station adds little coverage: KDSA alone covers 0.3–0.4 % of
the Earth-visible hours (hours when no DSN complex sees the Moon), e.g. 123 h in 5 years (≈25 h per year) for an
always-Earth-facing site. Its value is redundancy and a second antenna in the Asia–Pacific longitude band (KDSA sees
the Moon 41.6 % of all hours, DSN Canberra 41.9 %). Far-side sites (Chang'e 4, Chang'e 6) never see the Earth, so no
direct link at all; south-polar sites see it only part of the time.

## Limits
- Geometry only: no link budget, no antenna time allocation, no KDSA mission constraints.
- Station Moon visibility uses the ellipsoidal horizon (no local terrain at the antenna) and hour marks for the 5-year table; the 30-day schedule uses minute steps with KDSA rise/set to 1 s.
- KDSA coordinates: NAIF `kdsa01.tf`; permission to use them is a pending question to the Seoul organisers.
- SHACKLETON_RIM is the Gläser et al. (2017) rim point S (-89.6866, -162.81) since card 15; its horizon is card 13's recomputation for that point.
