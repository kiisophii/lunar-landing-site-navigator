We built a web tool that answers two questions for a lunar landing site and date: can a lander see the Sun (power and heat), and can it see the Earth (direct-to-Earth radio link)? Planners, educators and the public pick sites from a cited catalogue of 33 Apollo, Luna, Chang'e, Chandrayaan, SLIM, CLPS and Artemis III candidate locations and compare them side by side. For each site the tool shows Sun elevation, time until sunset and Earthset, and, over a chosen period, the share of hours with sunlight, Earth visibility, both, and the longest dark spell. Terrain horizons come from NASA LOLA elevation models at up to 5 m near the south pole, with lunar curvature; Sun and Earth positions come from JPL ephemerides checked against JPL Horizons. We compare our results with NASA's published polar illumination maps [TBD: MAE/r]. A relay-tower study shows how a short mast can restore the Earth link where terrain blocks it [PENDING MERGE: s4-relay-tower-sua]. It is a comparison and re-planning tool, not a best-site picker.
